portfolios:
  defensive: "The only one of the five with no equity. Almost two thirds sit in cash-like and very short-dated fixed income, with a floating-rate fund as the largest holding, so rising rates barely affect it and can even lift its return. The rest is split between credit chosen for the quality of the companies and defensive alternatives whose results do not depend on the direction of markets."
  conservative: "The first step into equity: a quarter of the portfolio, with the same mix as the riskier portfolios (global index, value and emerging markets) and a small slice of absolute-return funds. The other three quarters stay in short-dated fixed income, credit and defensive alternatives, so the portfolio keeps the character of the Defensive one with an added growth engine."
  balanced: "Half equity, half fixed income and defensive alternatives. With half the portfolio already in equities, the floating-rate fund drops out and, within fixed income, credit gains weight over cash-like funds: fixed income stops being a parking place and seeks a return of its own."
  dynamic: "Three quarters in global, value and emerging-market equity, plus absolute-return funds. The remaining quarter in fixed income is mostly credit and acts as ballast; defensive alternatives carry a small weight."
  aggressive: "All equity, with no fixed income. It is the only one of the five with a technology fund, and its cushion is the equity absolute-return funds, which seek results whether markets rise or fall."
