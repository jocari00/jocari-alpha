The second quarter has been the mirror image of the first: the ceasefire between the United States and Iran has removed the risk premium from oil, and global equities close their best quarter since 2020, led by artificial intelligence and emerging markets.

## Markets

On 8 April Washington and Tehran agreed a two-week truce that has led to a ceasefire framework and the gradual reopening of Hormuz, although traffic is still well below pre-war levels. Brent, which rose above $120 in April, ends June at around $71–73.

Global equities have gained about 15% in the quarter. Emerging markets, up 24%, post their best quarter since 2009, driven by semiconductor makers in Korea and Taiwan; the Philadelphia semiconductor index has nearly doubled. Commodities have been the exception: they have given back much of their first-quarter gains, and gold has fallen close to 13%.

Central banks have not shared the optimism. Kevin Warsh took office as Fed chair on 22 May and, although he has kept rates at 3.50–3.75%, he has dropped the references to future cuts. The ECB has gone further and raised the deposit rate to 2.25% in June, its first hike since 2023. The dollar has strengthened against the euro.

## How the allocation has behaved

The roles have reversed from the first quarter. The engines of the rebound have been the funds most exposed to the artificial intelligence cycle: **Robeco QI Emerging Markets**, with its weight in Korea and Taiwan, and **Fidelity Global Technology**. The **MSCI World** has moved with the market.

The value funds, **Cobas** and **Azvalor**, have lagged: the fall in oil and commodities has weighed on their largest positions. Even so, thanks to the first quarter, Azvalor Internacional is up about 15% for the half year.

In fixed income, tighter spreads have favoured **flexible credit**, and high-yield bonds have had a good quarter. The ECB hike has barely affected the **short-duration** funds, which keep accruing coupon.
