The third quarter has brought the energy shock back. The collapse of the ceasefire between the United States and Iran has again cut off passage through Hormuz, oil has risen above $100 once more and central banks have responded by raising rates. Equities are holding up, but bonds are going through one of their worst stretches in years.

## Markets

Brent has gone from about $73 at the end of June to above $100 in September. With inflation back in focus, the Fed has raised rates for the first time since 2023, to 3.75–4.00%; the ECB has taken the deposit rate to 2.50%, and the Bank of Japan to 1.25%, all in September.

Bond yields have risen across almost every developed-market curve: the US 10-year has reached 5.29%, and French and German yields are at their highest in more than 15 years. Credit spreads have widened, especially in high yield.

Global equities close the quarter with modest gains, supported by solid earnings, although September was negative. Underneath there has been wide dispersion: energy has been the best sector, technology suffered in July on doubts about artificial intelligence spending and has since recovered on earnings, and Europe has lagged on fears of stagflation.

## How the allocation has behaved

The return of oil has again favoured the value funds with energy exposure, **Cobas** and **Azvalor**, although September has been a difficult month for value as a style. **Fidelity Global Technology** went through a soft patch in July and has since regained ground; **emerging markets** have felt the repricing of artificial intelligence valuations in Asia.

Fixed income is showing why the portfolios avoid long duration: the bond sell-off is hitting long maturities hardest, while the **liquidity and short-term fixed income** funds and the floating-rate fund have barely felt it. **Credit** has suffered more from the widening of spreads in September.

**Catastrophe bonds** (**Fermat**) have had a calm quarter: the US hurricane and Japanese typhoon season has so far caused no significant damage, which is exactly when this asset earns its premium without surprises.
