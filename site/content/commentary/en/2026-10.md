October has punished AI-linked technology and emerging markets, while oil kept climbing and high-quality government bonds regained their role as a safe haven. It is the first month of formal tracking, and all five portfolios reach the end of October ahead of their benchmark for the year to date.

The table above shows the year to 30 October, not the month: it is an estimated period, with today's weights held constant since 31 December. Live tracking starts on 30 October, and from November each note will show the month's return.

## Macro backdrop

Central banks have paused after September's hikes. The Fed held rates at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October, but two members voted for a hike and markets now price one in December. The ECB left the deposit rate at 2.50% after two hikes this year, and Christine Lagarde announced she will step down in February.

Oil has kept setting the pace: renewed tension in the Middle East took Brent to $118. In Europe, political and fiscal uncertainty in France weighed on the euro, which fell to $1.116 early in the month, its lowest in seventeen months.

## Fixed income

With technology stocks retreating, high-quality government bonds have again acted as a counterweight: German bonds gained 1.7% over the month and US Treasuries 2.4%. France was the exception. Its 10-year yield reached its highest since 2002 and the spread over Germany widened to levels not seen since 2012.

Investment-grade credit gained 1.3%, while European high yield lost 1.0%.

## Equity

Global equities fell, but the headline figure hides a sharp rotation. The S&P 500 lost 0.9% while its equal-weighted version gained 5.0%: the large technology companies dragged the index down on doubts about the payoff from AI spending. By sector, energy rose 12.8% and technology fell 7.0%. Value beat growth by almost seven points.

Emerging markets were the worst market, down 8%: Korea and Taiwan suffered from the AI repricing in Asia, and Brazil slumped after its election.

## Alternatives

Hurricane Isaias made landfall in Florida on 9 October as a Category 2 storm, with losses below the threshold of most catastrophe bonds. Insured natural-catastrophe losses for the first nine months have been the lowest since 2019.

## The portfolios

All five portfolios are ahead of their benchmark for the year to date, by between 1.3 and 2.0 points, and the gap grows with the equity weight. Each block has its own reason behind that edge.

**Short-term fixed income.** It adds little, and the cause is rising rates. With the ECB's two hikes this year, bond prices have fallen: even at short maturities, that fall has eaten much of the coupon. The fixed-income benchmark, 1-3 year government and corporate bonds, has suffered the same, so this block is roughly level with it. Within it, the floating-rate fund, **DWS Floating Rate Notes**, has fared best, because its coupons reset higher with rates.

**Credit and flexible fixed income.** It adds because its managers are free to choose issuers, maturities and, in **Gamma Global**'s case, some equity, while the benchmark is tied to 1-3 year bonds. **Gamma Global** and **Man Global Investment Grade Opportunities** have more than offset a weaker year for **Carmignac Portfolio Credit**.

**Defensive alternatives.** This is the block that adds most in the cautious portfolios, because its return does not depend on rates. **Catastrophe bonds** (**Fermat**) have benefited from a hurricane season with few losses, and **Helium** and **DNCA Alpha Bonds** have earned positive returns from absolute-return strategies that depend little on market direction.

**Aggressive alternatives.** **Jupiter Merian** and **BlackRock Asia Pacific Absolute Return** do not depend on whether equities rise or fall, but on their managers' skill in choosing what to buy and what to sell. They are doing well this year: both show positive returns for the year to date, with little relation to what the market has done. Relative to the benchmark, however, they detract: there they count as equity, and global equities have risen more than they have this year.

**Equity.** The index fund, **Fidelity MSCI World**, is slightly behind the benchmark's MSCI ACWI because it leaves out emerging markets, which have risen more than the developed world. The value funds, **Azvalor** and **Cobas**, are the largest source of the edge in the equity-heavy portfolios: with oil above $100 since September, their energy and commodity exposure has pulled them up, and in October they were again the funds that rose the most. **Robeco QI Emerging Markets** is also well ahead of the world index for the year, despite October's fall, thanks to its gains over the first nine months. Technology barely departs from the index.

## What we are watching

The Fed meeting on 9 December, with new projections and a possible hike; the ECB in December and the succession to Lagarde; the French budget and the spread over Germany; oil and the situation in Hormuz; and whether the correction in AI-linked technology stays an adjustment or goes further.
